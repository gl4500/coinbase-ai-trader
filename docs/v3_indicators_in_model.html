IV. Bottom line

The v3 model is 50% a price/volume/range model and 30% a regime-conditioning model. Classical TA — the kind covered in the indicators field guide — contributes only the remainder, and only via two channels: RSI(14) and ADX(14).

The textbook story for an algorithmic trader is that the indicator zoo gives the model edge. Here the booster's gain breakdown disagrees: raw close, raw volume, and bar range alone account for ~50% of all split-gain, and the macro/external regime channels (funding rate, BTC correlation, IV/RV spread) account for another ~30%. The classical TA channels are mostly never split on, even though they were wired in.

Three implications for tuning:

  • Ten of the sixteen classical indicators are in the model but contribute zero gain. EMA, MACD, Bollinger, Stoch RSI, OBV, MFI, VWAP, ROC, and the candle-derived ch10/ch11 all show splits = 0. Either the trees never improved by splitting on them, or higher-rank features (raw price/volume) already captured the same signal.
  • RSI is the only classical oscillator that matters, at 5.6% of gain — and only via 4 total splits across 200 trees. ADX(14) is the surprise workhorse at 10.5%.
  • Half the model lives outside classical TA. Funding rate, BTC return correlation, and the IV/RV20 spread together dominate ADX. These are the regime signals the v3 design committed to in #311-refactor.

V. Pattern crosswalk — can v3 see the §VII patterns?

The 10 patterns from technical_indicators.html §VII are a human-trader vocabulary. This section checks each one against the live v3 booster's gain breakdown: does the model actually use the ingredients the pattern requires? Visibility is graded from the booster's perspective, not whether the pattern is profitable.

Read it this way. Of the 10 textbook setups, v3 has direct visibility into two at the ingredient level — the ones built on RSI and ADX. Five it is fully blind to, because every ingredient is either zero-gain or absent. Two it has half of (one ingredient lights up), and one (divergence) it could in principle learn via tree-path interactions even without a dedicated feature.

The model's actual decision surface is not the §VII pattern vocabulary. Of the 80% of total gain coming from raw-price/volume and macro-regime channels, none appears as an ingredient in any §VII pattern. v3 looks at the market in a different language — closer to "where does close sit inside its 60-bar range, conditioned on funding-rate regime" than to "is the BB squeezing and OBV ticking up." Neither view is wrong; they are different vocabularies.
