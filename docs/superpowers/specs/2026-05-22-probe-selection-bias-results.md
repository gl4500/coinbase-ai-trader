## What AUC is, and what we're measuring

**AUC = Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve.** A single
number summarizing how well a binary classifier *ranks* positive examples
above negatives.

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1.0 | Perfect — every positive scores higher than every negative |
| 0.5 | Random — coin-flip ranking; the model knows nothing |
| 0.0 | Perfectly *backwards* — fix by inverting the score |

**Intuitive reading.** AUC is the probability that a randomly picked positive
sample gets a higher model score than a randomly picked negative sample. So
AUC 0.512 = 51.2% of the time; AUC 0.55 (the project's production gate) =
55%; AUC 0.60+ = strong by financial-ML standards; AUC 0.65+ = very strong,
rare in noisy markets.

The probes in this project predict whether a triple-barrier UP outcome
fires (or, for v3, `close[t+k] > close[t]`). Every number in the deflation
table below is being compared against the null hypothesis **AUC = 0.5** —
a model that knows nothing.

The diagnostic does **not** measure profitability, calibration, or fee
impact — only ranking quality. (That's why the deployment scorecard exists
separately — see Session 58.71l: v3 fails 3 of 4 deployment gates even with
its small AUC edge.)

## The two noise scales

Every deflation cell below is anchored on one of two standard errors (SE) of
the AUC under the null:

| Noise model | SE | What it assumes |
|---|---|---|
| **iid** | 0.00141 | Every one of the ~167k cache samples is an independent draw. Textbook AUC variance. |
| **fold** (honest) | 0.00899 | The empirical spread of v3's AUC across the 5 purged-WF folds: `[0.516, 0.507, 0.527, 0.523, 0.529]`. |

**The fold SE is 6.4× larger than the iid SE** because triple-barrier labels
at a 4h horizon on hourly bars overlap ~75% with their neighbours — the
*effective* sample size is far below 167k. The fold SE measures what the
AUC actually does on genuinely time-separated data; iid is the textbook
mistake Bailey & López de Prado (AFML) call out.

Every iid row in the table below pegs at 1.000. Read those as a contrast
exhibit — "what naive significance testing would tell you, which is wrong
for overlapping financial labels" — not as the verdict.

## Findings, plain English

### Track A.1 — v3's best documented AUC (0.5284): MARGINAL

This is the best result the entire feature search ever produced — top-40
features by gain, tuned hyperparameters. Under the honest fold-level SE:

| N (search size) | exp_max under null | deflated_prob | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| 17 | 0.51643 | **0.908** | Almost clears the 0.95 bar. Real, but borderline. |
| 100 (headline) | 0.52275 | **0.735** | **Marginal.** Above the floor, short of confidence. |
| 200 | 0.52486 | **0.653** | Still above the floor — but weak. |

The project's best cherry-picked, tuned configuration is *probably* real
signal. But once you account for the ~100 trials behind it (channel
probes + 81-config hyperparameter grid + horizon sweep), it doesn't clear
the 0.95 confidence bar that would justify "deploy with conviction."

iid column for the same observed value: 1.000 across all N. Naive
significance testing says "14σ above chance, ship it." Wrong, for the
non-IID reasons in the noise-scales section above.

### Track A.2 — v3's honest deployable AUC (0.5120): BELOW the noise floor

The scorecard's purged-WF OOF AUC — what v3 actually delivers when measured
properly, not cherry-picked.

| N | exp_max under null | deflated_prob | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| 17 | 0.51643 | **0.311** | Below the floor. |
| 100 | 0.52275 | **0.116** | Well below. |
| 200 | 0.52486 | **0.076** | Indistinguishable from a noisy random search. |

The gap between 0.5284 (marginal) and 0.5120 (noise) is **0.0164 of AUC that
is selection + tuning artifact**, not real predictive signal. When you remove
the cherry-picking, what's left looks like search luck.

### Track B — "the only confirmed PASS" (RSI-rank +0.0124): BELOW the noise floor

RSI-rank survivorship-aware Δ = +0.0124 was logged as the single positive
finding from 16 channel candidates. Against null Δ = 0, fold SE:

| N | exp_max under null | observed Δ | deflated_prob | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 17 | **+0.01643** | +0.0124 | **0.327** | Below the floor. |
| 100 | +0.02275 | +0.0124 | **0.125** | Well below. |
| 200 | +0.02486 | +0.0124 | **0.083** | Basically noise. |

If you ran 17 channel-add probes on a *true-null* model (no real signal
anywhere), the *best* one you'd find by chance would average **+0.0164**.
RSI-rank's "win" is **+0.0124 — below that floor**. The "one confirmed
PASS" is almost certainly selection noise.

### Why the iid column is 1.000 everywhere

Every iid row pegs at 1.000 — including the v3 OOF AUC of 0.5120 (which is
actually noise) and RSI-rank's +0.0124 (also actually noise).

The reason iid is so badly wrong: 167k labels with 4h-horizon triple-barrier
windows on 1h bars are not independent draws. The textbook AUC variance
under-counts the noise by ~6×. Every "the new channel improved AUC by
+0.003 with p < 0.0001" claim made on overlapping financial labels is
*systematically over-confident*. This isn't a quirk of one calculation —
it's the textbook example of why financial ML needs special treatment.

## What this changes for the roadmap

Three things are simultaneously true:

1. **There IS a small real edge in v3.** The headline 0.735 isn't zero — at
   N=17 fold the cherry-picked best (0.5284) hits 0.908, close to clearing
   the bar. *Something* learned from the data.
2. **The honest model is noise.** The actual deployed OOF AUC (0.5120)
   deflates to 0.116 at N=100 fold. Once you remove cherry-picking, what's
   left looks like search luck.
3. **The "confirmed wins" probably weren't.** RSI-rank's +0.0124 lift is
   below the best-of-N noise floor. The 16-failed-1-passed narrative is
   plausibly "16 probes failed, 1 looked good by chance."

**Implications:**

- The scorecard's "1 of 4 gates" verdict (Session 58.71l) gets sharper. v3
  isn't just "not deployable after fees" — the underlying ranking signal
  isn't even statistically separable from search noise *before* fees enter
  the picture.
- **The case for more single-add channel probes on this cache is dead.**
  That gate cannot be cleared honestly. The best you could reasonably
  expect is another result that *looks* good but doesn't survive deflation.
- **Structural-change paths win by comparison.** The bar-structure
  exploration (SP1/SP2, shipped — Sessions 58.71n–o) is exactly this kind
  of pivot — change the data, not just the features. **#20 CUSUM event
  filters** and **#23 regime-conditional boosters** are similarly
  structural and remain defensible.
- **The cheap hygiene moves are essentially mandatory.** **#17 CPCV** and
  **#18 sample-uniqueness weighting** aren't "polish on a working system" —
  they're "stop overstating the signal you have." Especially #18 — the
  non-IID correction the iid-vs-fold gap quantified.
- **#22 DoubleEnsemble and #24 GMADL are less attractive.** Both layer
  machinery on a primary whose signal is fragile; they'd inherit the
  fragility.

**Honest read for what to do next.** Don't pour weeks into another single-add
channel probe on this cache. Either go structural — and wait for the SP1/SP2
operator-run verdict to tell you if dollar bars beat time bars — or accept
that v3 is "slightly better than coin-flip" and ship/decommission it on
that framing.
